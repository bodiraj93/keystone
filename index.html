Kei Trucks & Fleets · 軽トラ

Kei trucks, landed and plated in Manitoba.

Japan's 660cc working truck: enclosed heated cab, true four-wheel drive with low range, and a steel bed that dumps on some models. Built by Suzuki, Daihatsu, Honda, Subaru, and Mitsubishi to one national standard, and street legal here once Manitoba plates it.

The brief

What most buyers here don't know yet.

What a kei truck is

Kei-class trucks are Japan's smallest legal truck category: 660cc engines, about 3.4 metres long and 1.5 metres wide, built to a strict national standard refined since the 1950s. Every major Japanese maker builds one, and the Suzuki Carry, Daihatsu Hijet, Honda Acty, Subaru Sambar, and Mitsubishi Minicab account for millions of units.

They exist because Japan's farms, fisheries, and city trades needed a complete working truck that fits where cars don't. Decades of competition went into exactly that problem.

Capability, on paper

Selectable four-wheel drive with high and low range is common; many trucks add a rear differential lock and an ultra-low crawler gear for working under load. Rated payload is about 350 kg (770 lb) on a flat steel bed with fold-down sides, and a 4-foot sheet of plywood lies flat with the tailgate down.

Factory configurations include hydraulic dump beds, scissor-lift beds, and fully enclosed van bodies.

Size is the feature

A kei truck is narrower than most UTVs and shorter than a compact car, with a turning circle to match. It fits through a standard gate, down a shelterbelt or orchard row, into a barn alley, and into the corner of a garage.

The difference from a side-by-side is what happens when the work is done: you drive it to town on a plate.

Running costs and service

The engines are small, simple gasoline units that burn 5 to 7 litres per 100 km of regular fuel. Consumables are ordinary: filters, belts, plugs, and brake parts are stocked or a short order away, and any independent shop can service one. Every handover includes a parts source list, and parts ride the same container as the trucks.

Winter operation

Low curb weight over four driven wheels gives a traction-to-weight ratio full-size trucks can't match, and low range keeps it controllable in deep snow. The cab is heated, with a real defroster and wipers, which is the part open machines cannot answer in January.

A mounted winter wheel set can be added to any build and is priced with your quote.

Why Japan's used trucks arrive in this condition

Japan's mandatory shaken inspection makes deferred maintenance expensive, so trucks are either kept up or retired early. Rural mileage is short-haul and low, many regions never salt their roads, and every auction listing carries an inspector's grade and condition map before a single bid is placed. These trucks come out of a system built to document condition.

Ownership in Manitoba

Vehicles 15 years and older enter Canada exempt from RIV compliance. After an out-of-province safety inspection, MPI registers, insures, and plates a kei truck like any other vehicle, and the document binder handed over with the truck contains everything the Autopac agent needs.

Farm & Heavy Machinery · 農機

Low-hour Japanese tractors, landed on your yard.

Japan's farms retire equipment early and maintain it obsessively. Sourcing runs through Japan's dealer-only machinery auctions: Kubota, Yanmar, and Iseki, bought at hammer price and shipped to Manitoba.

Hours, not years

Japanese compact tractors are graded on hours and condition, not age. Machines regularly come to auction with low hours, documented condition reports, and the kind of maintenance small farms in Japan are known for. They're mechanically simple diesels, the sort of equipment that outlives its paperwork.

The brief

Know the machine before it ships.

Why low-hour machines surface in Japan

Small Japanese farms replace equipment on cycles set by inspection culture and upgrade programs, not by wear. Machines are stored indoors, worked on small acreages, and come to auction with a few hundred to a couple thousand hours, each carrying a graded condition report and photographs taken before bidding opens.

What lands well in Manitoba

Compact diesels from 15 to 45 hp cover most acreage work: loader duty, tilling, mowing, pallet forks, and post-hole augers. Japanese-market tractors commonly run standard Category 1 three-point hitches and rear PTO, so North American implements mount with little or no adaptation.

Rear-mounted PTO snowblowers are standard equipment in Japan's snow country, and they pair naturally with a Manitoba winter.

Parts and compatibility

Kubota, Yanmar, and Iseki all carry deep parts networks in North America, and many Japan-market models share engines and driveline components with machines sold here. Where a model is Japan-only, that is flagged before you bid, and its parts ride the same container as everything else.

Decals and manuals arrive in Japanese; operating references in English exist for the common platforms.

Mini excavators and other machinery

One-to-three-tonne excavators, tillers, track carriers, and implements move through the same auctions. Off-road equipment carries no age restriction and needs no roadworthiness certificate to work your land, and farm tractors typically enter duty-free under their own tariff classification.

Reading a machinery listing

Hours matter less than the match between hours and condition. Listings are screened for consistent wear, intact undercarriages and hydraulics, and photographs that support the meter. Anything that fails that reading never reaches your inbox.

Off-road means fewer rules, and I know which ones

✓ Off-road: tractors & excavators

No age limit. Machinery enters Canada as industrial equipment: no 15-year rule, no RIV headache, no roadworthiness certificate to use it on your land, no $100 A/C excise. Farm tractors generally enter duty-free under their own tariff class (HS 87.01). Your written quote itemizes exactly what applies to your machine.

On-road, for contrast: kei trucks & cars

Road vehicles are bound by the 15-year rule for RIV exemption and need an out-of-province safety before MPI plates them. The two categories are quoted differently here because the rules are different, and the difference shows up in your landed cost.

What one costs

Machinery freight varies more than vehicles; size and weight set the shipping, so every unit is quoted before you bid. The structure is always the same: auction price + Japan-side fees and crating + ocean freight + GST at the border, plus my flat $2,450 service fee.

JDM Enthusiast · 名車

The cars Japan kept to itself, now fifteen years old and fair game.

Canada's 15-year rule opens the door; the auction sheet decides what's worth walking through it for. Every candidate is sourced sheet-first, translated, and landed in Winnipeg.

Newly eligible: refreshed every January

New for 2026: the 2011 model year. Every January another year of Japan's catalogue becomes importable; this year that means machines like the Delica D:5, Alphard and Vellfire, Elgrand, and Jimny join the list.

The brief

What the door opens onto.

What the 15-year rule unlocks

Canada admits vehicles 15 years and older without RIV compliance, which opens the half of Japan's catalogue that was never sold here: Delica and Hiace vans, Alphard and Elgrand people-movers, Jimny 4x4s, kei sports cars, Skylines, Crowns, and the rest of the machinery Japan built for itself.

Every January another model year crosses the line, which is why serious buyers plan a season ahead.

Why auction buying beats a dealer lot

Japan's auction system runs on independent inspection. Every vehicle carries a numeric grade, a panel-by-panel condition map, and mileage checked against national records, all published before bidding. You bid on documented condition rather than a seller's description, and the sheet travels with the car. The Auction Guide shows how to read one.

Right-hand drive, day to day

MPI insures right-hand-drive vehicles like any other. The cluster already reads in km/h and metric, and the controls fall to hand within a day of driving. Model quirks are identified before you bid: The FM radio band differs on many models, for example, and a band expander or a new head unit settles it.

Winter and rust

Screening happens at buy time: underbody photographs and the sheet's corrosion notes come before any bid, so the car starts clean. From there it is treated like any vehicle worth keeping; rustproofing before the first winter is the standard recommendation and can be booked alongside the safety work.

Parts afterward

Mechanical parts for mainstream JDM platforms are widely available in North America, and model-specific pieces come through the same dismantler network as the panels and wheels on the Parts page, riding container rates instead of courier pricing.

Parts & Accessories · 部品

The parts too big to ship, until they ride in my container.

Panels, engines, transmissions, wheel sets: the stuff couriers punish you for. Sourced from Japan's auction and dismantler networks, landed on container rates.

The brief

How bulky parts actually move.

Where the parts come from

Japan's dismantler network parts out low-mileage, inspection-retired vehicles by the thousands. Panels arrive straight and unrusted, interiors arrive complete, and drivetrains arrive with documented donor mileage. Auction-yard surplus covers wheels, accessories, and new-old stock.

Why container freight wins on bulk

Couriers price by dimensional weight, which punishes anything large and light: a bumper cover, a hood, a set of wheels. Container space is priced as a share of a crossing already booked, so bulky parts land for a fraction of a courier quote and clear customs on the same entry as the vehicles.

What to send for a quote

A part number is fastest. Otherwise, clear photographs and the donor vehicle's model and year. The quote comes back landed, with the source invoice attached, and if the part exists in Japan's network the answer is a number; if it doesn't, the answer is a fast no.

Fitment and condition

You confirm fitment; condition is confirmed here with photographs before anything ships. Used parts sell as they come, documented, and Japan-only parts have their differences flagged before you commit.

What ships well:
  • Body panels, bumpers, hoods, and doors
  • Engines and transmissions
  • Wheel and tire sets
  • Seats and interior pieces
  • Anything heavy, bulky, or awkward.
Small parts? Use a parcel proxy.

For anything that fits in a shoebox, Japan's parcel-proxy services are faster and cheaper, and you should use one. Container freight earns its keep on parts that couriers price by volume.

Full FAQ

Is this legal? How does the 15-year rule work?
Yes. Vehicles 15 years or older from their build date are exempt from Canada's RIV compliance program and can be imported and registered. Off-road machinery like tractors isn't bound by the 15-year rule at all. The entry is filed through a licensed customs broker with you as importer of record; CBSA clears it; Manitoba plates it.
How do I register and insure it in Manitoba?
After the safety inspection, you take the document binder I give you to any Autopac agent; they register, insure, and plate it in one visit. RST is paid there. The walkthrough is part of every handover, and the safety can be booked on your behalf.
What if it arrives damaged?
Every shipment carries marine insurance, and the vehicle is photographed at every stage (auction yard, export dock, loading, and arrival), so its condition is documented end to end. If something happens on the water, the claim has a paper trail behind it.
What taxes and duties will I pay?
5% GST at the border on the vehicle's value plus any duty. Japan-built vehicles qualify for 0% duty under the CPTPP trade agreement when the shipment carries a Certificate of Origin; I guarantee one with every Japan-built vehicle, and if a vehicle was built outside Japan I flag the duty before you bid. Duties, GST, and port fees are paid by you, pre-paid before the container is released. Manitoba RST is paid when you register. There's also a $100 federal excise if the vehicle has A/C.
Can I see the vehicle before bidding?
You see everything that exists: the translated auction sheet, the inspector's grading, and yard photos before any bid. You set a written maximum, and I can't exceed it. The one thing nobody can offer on an auction car is a test drive; the sheet system exists because of that, and the Auction Guide shows you how I read one.
How long does it take?
Typically 10–16 weeks door-to-door, most of which is ocean and port time. Shipping schedules are estimates; delays at the Port of Vancouver or on CN Rail can stretch them, so your quote includes a dated estimate and you get live tracking every step of the way.
What about parts and service afterward?
Parts flow through the same channel as the vehicles, and bulky items ride cheap on container rates. Every handover includes a parts source list, and you can send me a parts request anytime.
